# XDSAAI Placement Readiness — Professional Do's & Don'ts

## 1. Self-Introduction

### Do
- Start with a confident greeting and your name.
- Follow a logical flow: education → experience/internship → projects → skills → role relevance → closing.
- Explain what you actually worked on, not only what the project was about.
- Mention tools, methods and measurable outcomes when they are genuine.
- Keep the introduction structured and interview-focused.
- Practise aloud so your delivery sounds natural rather than memorized.

### Don't
- Do not read the introduction word-for-word from a screen.
- Do not claim skills you cannot explain with an example.
- Do not invent companies, project ownership, metrics or responsibilities.
- Do not speak for too long without connecting your experience to the target role.
- Do not criticize previous employers, trainers or team members.

## 2. Resume

### Do
- Keep the resume concise, readable and role-relevant.
- Use clear section headings and consistent formatting.
- Write achievement-oriented bullets using action + work + outcome where possible.
- Keep dates, job titles and project information consistent across profiles.
- Proofread grammar, spelling, contact details and links.
- Keep a master resume and tailor the relevant version for each target role.

### Don't
- Do not copy another candidate's resume content.
- Do not use fake experience, certifications, employers or percentages.
- Do not overload the resume with every technology you have ever seen.
- Do not use long paragraphs for project or experience descriptions.
- Do not include confidential client, employer or personal information.

## 3. LinkedIn & Naukri

### Do
- Use a professional headline aligned with the role you are targeting.
- Keep your About, Skills, Projects and Experience sections consistent with your resume.
- Add relevant projects and evidence of practical work.
- Keep job preferences and profile visibility settings appropriate for your search.
- Review your profile regularly for outdated information.

### Don't
- Do not inflate job titles or years of experience.
- Do not add skills simply because they appear in a job description.
- Do not publish confidential project information.
- Do not use an unprofessional profile photo or incomplete contact information.

## 4. Projects & Technical Discussion

### Do
- Be ready to explain the problem, dataset, approach, tools, challenges and outcome.
- Know why you selected a particular model, technology or architecture.
- Explain your individual contribution clearly when the project was team-based.
- Be honest about what you implemented versus what you studied.

### Don't
- Do not memorize definitions without understanding them.
- Do not claim end-to-end ownership if you worked on only one component.
- Do not hide limitations or known issues; explain how you would improve them.

## 5. Interview Communication

### Do
- Listen fully before answering.
- Take a short pause when you need to organize your thoughts.
- Use professional phrases such as “In my previous project…”, “My contribution was…”, and “The key challenge was…”.
- Ask a relevant clarification question when the requirement is unclear.
- Close answers confidently and concisely.

### Don't
- Do not interrupt the interviewer.
- Do not use excessive filler words such as “actually”, “basically” and “like”.
- Do not argue when you receive corrective feedback.
- Do not give a one-word answer when an example would demonstrate your ability.

## Final 60-Second Quality Gate

Before an interview, confirm:

- [ ] Resume is updated and proofread.
- [ ] Naukri profile matches the resume.
- [ ] LinkedIn profile matches the resume.
- [ ] Introduction is practised without reading.
- [ ] Top 2–3 projects can be explained end-to-end.
- [ ] Every listed skill can be supported with a practical example.
- [ ] All claims are truthful and explainable.
